About the workshop

The workshop will focus on the formation and evolution of globular clusters (GCs) and the connections with the assembly of the Milky Way and galaxies at high redshift. This is both relevant and timely as the topic has experienced a recent surge of interest. Gaia has been transformational for our understanding cluster formation, GC kinematics, and Milky Way dynamics. Remarkable observations from JWST have also revealed GC-like compact sources in lensed high-redshift galaxies, making up a significant fraction of the galaxy light and star formation. This opens a new window into understanding GC and galaxy formation. We hope that the workshop will encourage connections between researchers across these areas of investigation.

We solicit abstracts for the program of talks (~15-minute talks or ~5-min flash talks) and suggestions of discussion topics, and welcome contributions on a wide range of topics related to globular clusters and their host galaxy, for example:

  • the formation of globular clusters from giant molecular clouds
  • the impact of the stellar feedback from massive stars in clusters onto the ISM
  • compact objects, black holes in globular clusters as sources of gravitational waves
  • stellar streams from GCs and their use as probes of dark matter
  • the role of globular clusters as tracers of galaxy assembly and star formation
  • the connection between GCs in the Milky Way and in high-redshift lensed galaxies
The workshop builds off a series of successful previous workshops (McMaster 2022, Michigan 2023, Toronto 2024) which have strengthened regional connections and offered new networking opportunities, especially for early-career researchers.

Some partial travel support will be available for early-career scientists to offset the costs of accommodation in Halifax. There is no registration fee for this workshop.

Code of conduct

All attendees, speakers, and organizers at our workshop are expected to adhere to the following code of conduct. We strive to create a safe, inclusive, and respectful environment for everyone involved. We encourage constructive discussions and debates, emphasizing the criticism of ideas rather than individuals.

The "Globular Clusters: From the Milky Way to High-Redshift Galaxies" workshop is committed to providing a workshop experience free from harassment for all participants, regardless of sex, gender identity and expression, age, sexual orientation, disability, physical appearance, neurodiversity, body size, race, ethnicity, religion (or lack thereof). We have a zero-tolerance policy for any form of harassment towards workshop participants. This includes the prohibition of sexual language, explicit imagery, or any behavior that may be deemed offensive in workshop venues, including talks, discussion sessions, social events, and online platforms.

Harassment includes, but is not limited to, making offensive verbal comments related to sex, gender identity and expression, age, sexual orientation, disability, physical appearance, body size, race, ethnicity, religion, displaying sexual images in public spaces, engaging in deliberate intimidation, stalking, sustained disruption of talks or other events, inappropriate physical contact, and unwelcome sexual attention.

Participants who are asked to cease any form of harassing behavior must comply immediately. In the event that a participant engages in harassment, the workshop organizers reserve the right to take appropriate action, which may include issuing a warning, expulsion from the workshop, or any other measure they deem necessary.

If you experience or witness any form of harassment, or if you have any other concerns regarding the workshop, please contact the scientific organizers. We are here to support you and address any issues that arise.

Together, let's make this workshop a welcoming and inclusive event for all participants.

Code of conduct adapted from the "Globular Clusters and Their Tidal Tails 2024" workshop and the "Great Lakes Clusters and Streams 2023" Conference.
